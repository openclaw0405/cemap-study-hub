9. Permanent Interest Bearing Shares (PIBS)

Issued by building societies to raise capital. Despite the name, they function like bonds.

  • Fixed interest rate, paid regularly like a bond coupon
  • No maturity date — perpetual (hence 'permanent')
  • Traded on the London Stock Exchange
  • Subordinated: in a winding-up, PIBS holders rank below ordinary depositors — high credit risk
  • Price is very sensitive to interest rate movements (like long-dated bonds)
  • FSCS protection: the interest payments may be covered, but PIBS holders do NOT get FSCS protection for capital loss — they rank as creditors, not depositors
Exam Trap: PIBS are shares in name but behave like fixed interest securities. They are NOT protected by the FSCS against capital loss. Do not confuse them with ordinary building society savings accounts.

10. Child Trust Fund (CTF)

  • Government scheme — closed to new accounts since 2 January 2011
  • Replaced by Junior ISA for new savers
  • Existing CTF accounts continue and can be transferred to a Junior ISA
  • Initial government contributions: £250 for most children; £500 for lower-income families
  • Parents and family could contribute up to the annual limit
  • Funds locked until the child reaches age 18
  • Two types: Cash CTF or Stocks & Shares CTF
Key exam point: CTF is closed to new entrants. Junior ISA is its replacement. Existing CTF holders can transfer to a Junior ISA but cannot hold both simultaneously.

11. Government's Help to Save

Targeted savings account for people on low incomes claiming Universal Credit or Working Tax Credit.

Exam Trap: The bonus is calculated on the highest balance, not the balance at the end. Withdrawals reduce the highest balance and therefore the bonus — this is the key trap.

12. Annuities

An annuity converts a lump sum (typically a pension fund) into a guaranteed income. Once purchased, the decision is irreversible.

Types of Annuity
  • Level: fixed income throughout — eroded by inflation over time
  • Escalating: income increases each year (e.g., 3%/year fixed or RPI-linked)
  • With-profits: income varies with investment performance
  • Enhanced / impaired life: higher income for those with health conditions or lifestyle factors
  • Joint life: continues (at reduced rate) to surviving spouse/partner
Key Concepts
  • Open Market Option (OMO): right to buy an annuity from ANY provider, not just your existing pension provider. Essential to maximise income.
  • Annuity rate: affected by age, gender (now unisex), health, gilt yields, provider, type chosen
  • Guarantee period: annuity pays for minimum guaranteed period (e.g., 5/10 years) even if annuitant dies earlier
  • Capital protection: returns remaining fund value on death
Open Market Option is examinable: A client should always exercise the OMO — shopping around can significantly increase retirement income. Failure to advise the OMO is a major regulatory failing.

Corporate Actions

  • Rights issue: company offers new shares to existing shareholders at a discount to market price — raises new capital
  • Bonus/scrip issue: free additional shares issued from reserves — no new cash raised; dilutes share price proportionally
  • Share buy-back: company repurchases own shares — reduces shares in issue, increases EPS
Limited liability: ordinary shareholders can only lose the amount they invested. Company debts cannot be claimed against personal assets (unlike sole trader/partnership).

14. Derivatives

Financial contracts whose value derives from an underlying asset (shares, currencies, commodities, interest rates).

Futures
  • Obligation to buy/sell the underlying asset at a set price on a future date
  • Binding on both parties
  • Used for hedging (protecting against price changes) or speculation
  • Traded on exchanges (standardised contracts)
Options
  • Right but not obligation to buy (call option) or sell (put option)
  • Buyer pays a premium for this right
  • Exercise price = strike price
  • American option: exercisable any time before expiry
  • European option: exercisable only at expiry
CFDs (Contracts for Difference)
  • Profit/loss = difference in asset price from open to close
  • No ownership of underlying asset
  • Margined product — leverage magnifies gains AND losses
  • Not suitable for most retail clients
Hedging vs Speculation
  • Hedging: using derivatives to reduce existing risk exposure
  • Speculation: using derivatives to take on risk for potential profit
  • Same instrument, opposite purpose
Exam Trap: Futures create an OBLIGATION; options give a RIGHT but no obligation. An option buyer's maximum loss is limited to the premium paid. A futures position has unlimited potential loss.

15. Sharesave and Share Option Schemes

SchemeKey features
SAYE (Sharesave)Save £5–£500/month for 3 or 5 years. Option to buy employer shares at up to 20% discount to market price at scheme start. At maturity: use savings to buy shares at option price OR take savings as cash. No income tax on the discount at purchase if HMRC-approved.
CSOP (Company Share Option Plan)Options to buy shares at market value. Limit: £60,000 of options per employee. No income tax on exercise if held for 3 years. CGT on eventual disposal.
EMI (Enterprise Management Incentives)For smaller companies. Up to £250,000 of options per employee. Very tax-efficient. Income tax and CGT advantages.
SIP (Share Incentive Plan)Free shares, partnership shares (bought from pre-tax salary), matching shares. Held in trust for minimum period.

16. Structured Products

Investment products whose return is linked to the performance of an underlying index (e.g., FTSE 100), with a defined term (typically 3–7 years).

Capital-Protected (PGPs)
  • Capital returned at maturity regardless of index performance
  • Plus potential upside (e.g., 100% of FTSE growth, capped)
  • Lower potential return for the protection
  • Protection only valid if held to maturity AND issuer doesn't default
Capital-at-Risk Products
  • Capital at risk if index falls below a defined barrier (e.g., 50% barrier)
  • Higher potential return to compensate
  • Kick-out plans: early maturity if index hits target on annual measurement date
Exam Traps: (1) Counterparty risk: if the issuing bank defaults, the investor could lose all capital even in a 'capital-protected' product. (2) Early exit: breaking the product before maturity usually incurs significant penalties. (3) Participation rate: if the product offers only 80% of index growth, the investor does not get the full gain.

Practice Questions

Q1. Kezia is eligible for Help to Save. She saves the maximum £50/month for the full 4-year term without making any withdrawals. What is the total government bonus she will receive?
A. £600 — the bonus is only paid once at the end of year 4
B. £1,200 — 50% bonus paid in two stages based on highest balance
C. £1,440 — 60% bonus on total contributions of £2,400
D. £600 — 25% bonus paid at year 2 and 25% at year 4
Reveal answer
Answer: B — £1,200. Help to Save pays a 50% bonus at year 2 on the highest balance reached in years 1–2 (max £600 if balance = £1,200), and another 50% bonus at year 4 on the increase in highest balance from year 2 to year 4 (max further £600). Total max bonus = £1,200.
Q2. Which of the following best describes the difference between a futures contract and an options contract?
A. Futures can only be used for hedging; options can only be used for speculation
B. A futures contract creates an obligation to trade; an options contract gives the right but not the obligation to trade
C. Options are exchange-traded; futures are always over-the-counter
D. The maximum loss on a futures contract is limited to the initial margin
Reveal answer
Answer: B. This is the fundamental distinction. A futures contract is binding — both parties MUST complete the transaction at the agreed price and date. An options contract gives the buyer the right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) at the strike price; the buyer's maximum loss is the premium paid.
Q3. Mrs Tan is about to retire and convert her pension fund into an annuity with her existing pension provider. Her adviser must make her aware of what right?
A. The right to delay taking the annuity for up to 10 years
B. The Open Market Option — the right to purchase an annuity from any provider
C. The right to a tax-free lump sum of 100% of the fund
D. The right to a capital-guaranteed annuity at no extra cost
Reveal answer
Answer: B — the Open Market Option (OMO). Every individual with a defined contribution pension has the right to shop around and buy an annuity from any provider, not just their existing pension company. Failure to advise the OMO is a significant regulatory breach. Annuity rates vary significantly between providers, and enhanced annuities may offer much better income for those with health conditions.
