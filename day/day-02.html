8. Residence and Taxation

  • UK tax resident = taxed on worldwide income regardless of where it arises
  • Non-UK resident = taxed only on UK-source income
  • Residence is determined by the Statutory Residence Test (SRT) — automatic tests based on days spent in the UK, connection factors, and work patterns
  • Broadly: ≥183 days in the UK in a tax year → automatically UK resident; ≤15 days in UK → automatically non-resident
  • The tax year runs 6 April to 5 April
Remittance basis: Non-UK domiciled residents may elect to pay tax only on foreign income when it is remitted (brought into) the UK, rather than on arising. A charge applies for long-term residents.

9. Domicile and Taxation

  • Domicile is the country you consider your permanent home — different from residence (where you currently live)
  • Domicile of origin: acquired at birth (father's domicile if parents married)
  • Domicile of choice: established by living in a new country with the intention to remain permanently; requires deliberate act and intention
  • Domicile of dependence: follows the domicile of a parent (child under 16)
  • Non-domiciliaries may elect the remittance basis — taxed on foreign income only when brought to the UK
Exam Trap: You can be resident in the UK but domiciled elsewhere. These are legally separate concepts and have different tax consequences — especially for IHT.

10. Income Liable to Tax

All income falling into these categories is potentially liable to UK income tax:

  • Employment income — salary, wages, bonuses, commissions, benefits in kind
  • Self-employment income — profits from a trade, profession or vocation
  • Property income — rents from UK land and property
  • Savings income — interest from bank accounts, bonds, most fixed-interest securities
  • Dividend income — dividends from shares and investment funds
  • Pension income — State Pension, occupational pensions, annuities, drawdown income
  • Miscellaneous income — casual income, certain state benefits (e.g., Jobseeker's Allowance)
Not liable to income tax: FSCS payments, gambling winnings, gifts, ISA income and growth, Premium Bond prizes.

11. The UK Income Tax System

Tax year: 6 April to 5 April. Collection via:

PAYE (Pay As You Earn)
  • Operated by employers
  • Tax deducted from wages in real time
  • Tax code (e.g., 1257L) determines personal allowance
  • No tax return required for straightforward employment
Self Assessment
  • Required for self-employed, landlords, higher earners, directors
  • Register by 5 October after the tax year
  • Paper return: 31 October
  • Online return: 31 January following the tax year
  • Balancing payment due: 31 January
  • Payments on account: 31 January and 31 July

12. Categories of Income Tax (2024/25)

Income is taxed in a strict order: non-savings → savings → dividends. Each layer uses up the tax bands sequentially.

  • First £500 of dividend income: 0% (2024/25)
  • Applies regardless of tax band

  • Reduces by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000
  • Fully withdrawn at income of £125,140

13. Benefits in Kind (BiK)

  • Benefits provided by an employer in addition to (or instead of) cash salary are taxable as employment income
  • Reported on form P11D — employer submits annually; employee pays tax via PAYE code or self-assessment
  • Employer pays Class 1A NI (13.8%) on most BiKs
Taxable BiKs (common exam examples)
  • Company car (CO₂-based charge)
  • Private medical insurance
  • Low-interest or interest-free loans above £10,000
  • Non-business travel expenses
  • Living accommodation (unless job-related)
Exempt BiKs (not taxable)
  • Workplace canteen meals (available to all staff)
  • One mobile phone per employee
  • Annual staff party up to £150 per head
  • Pension contributions by employer
  • Childcare vouchers (legacy schemes)
  • Cycle-to-work scheme equipment

14. Steps in Calculating a Tax Bill

  1. Total gross income — add all income from all sources
  2. Deduct reliefs — e.g., Gift Aid donations (grossed up), pension contributions (relief at source adjustments)
  3. Net income
  4. Deduct Personal Allowance (£12,570; tapers above £100,000)
  5. Taxable income
  6. Apply rates in order: non-savings → savings → dividends against the tax bands
  7. Deduct tax reducers — e.g., Marriage Allowance credit (£252), Enterprise Investment Scheme relief
  8. Tax liability
Marriage Allowance: The lower-earning spouse can transfer £1,260 of their unused personal allowance to a basic-rate-taxpaying partner, saving up to £252 in tax. Not available if either spouse is a higher/additional rate taxpayer.

15. Payment of Income Tax

PAYE employees
  • Tax collected weekly/monthly by employer
  • Employer sends PAYE returns to HMRC in real time (RTI)
  • Over/under-deductions corrected at year end via P800
Self Assessment deadlines
  • 5 Oct: Register for SA
  • 31 Oct: Paper return due
  • 31 Jan: Online return + balancing payment + 1st payment on account
  • 31 Jul: 2nd payment on account
Payments on account: Required where tax liability exceeds £1,000 and less than 80% was collected via PAYE. Each POA = 50% of prior year's liability. Late payment penalty: 5% surcharge on unpaid tax after 30 days.

16. Taxation of Life Policies

Qualifying Policies
  • Regular premium policies held for ≥10 years (or ¾ of the policy term if shorter)
  • Maturity proceeds and death benefits are free of income tax and CGT
  • Examples: qualifying whole of life, qualifying endowments
Non-Qualifying Policies (Investment Bonds)
  • Single premium or not meeting qualifying rules
  • Subject to chargeable event gains at maturity, surrender, death, or assignment
  • 5% per annum cumulative tax-deferred withdrawal allowance
  • Top-slicing relief may reduce the higher-rate liability on a gain
Top-slicing relief: Divides the gain by the number of complete years the policy has been held. The sliced gain is added to other income — if it does not push the taxpayer into a higher band, no further tax is due. If it does, only the excess above the band boundary is taxed at the higher rate.

Memorise

Income Tax Bands 2024/25
  • PA: £12,570 (0%)
  • Basic: £12,571–£50,270 (20%)
  • Higher: £50,271–£125,140 (40%)
  • Additional: above £125,140 (45%)
  • PA tapers from £100,000 → gone at £125,140
Savings & Dividend Allowances
  • Savings starting rate: £5,000 at 0%
  • PSA: £1,000 (basic) / £500 (higher) / £0 (additional)
  • Dividend allowance: £500 at 0%
  • Dividend rates: 8.75% / 33.75% / 39.35%
Self-Assessment Key Dates
  • Register: 5 October
  • Paper return: 31 October
  • Online return: 31 January
  • Balancing payment: 31 January
  • 2nd POA: 31 July
Life Policy Tax
  • Qualifying → no income tax / CGT on proceeds
  • Non-qualifying → chargeable event gain taxed
  • 5% per year tax-deferred withdrawal
  • Top-slicing relief available

Practice Questions

Q1. Priya earns a salary of £55,000 in 2024/25. What rate of income tax will she pay on her savings interest income?
A. 0% — covered by the Personal Savings Allowance
B. 20% — basic rate
C. 40% — higher rate, with a £500 Personal Savings Allowance at 0%
D. 45% — additional rate
Reveal answer
Answer: C. Priya is a higher-rate taxpayer (income £55,000 exceeds the £50,270 basic-rate limit). Higher-rate taxpayers receive a PSA of £500 at 0%; savings interest above £500 is taxed at 40%.
Q2. An employer provides an employee with private medical insurance worth £600 per year. Which form is used to report this, and who submits it?
A. P60 — submitted by the employee
B. P45 — submitted by HMRC
C. P11D — submitted by the employer
D. P800 — submitted by the employee
Reveal answer
Answer: C — P11D submitted by the employer. Employers must submit P11Ds annually for employees who receive benefits in kind. Private medical insurance is a taxable benefit. The employer also pays Class 1A NI (13.8%) on its value.
Q3. James holds a non-qualifying investment bond for 8 years and then surrenders it, making a gain of £16,000. He is a higher-rate taxpayer. Which relief may reduce his liability?
A. Entrepreneurs' Relief
B. Top-slicing relief — dividing the gain by 8 years
C. The 5% tax-deferred withdrawal rule cancels all liability
D. No relief is available — the full gain is taxed at 45%
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Top-slicing relief. The gain (£16,000) is divided by the policy's duration (8 years), giving a slice of £2,000. If this slice does not take James above the higher-rate threshold, no higher-rate tax applies on that portion. The 5% rule allows ongoing withdrawals, not a tax-free lump sum.
