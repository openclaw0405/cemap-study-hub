1. Types of Client

Retail client
  • Highest protection
  • Default for individuals
  • Consumer Duty applies
Professional client
  • Less protection
  • Typically large/corporate/institutional with expertise
Eligible counterparty
  • Lowest protection
  • Used for wholesale market dealings

2. Vulnerable Clients — do the right thing

  • Vulnerability can be temporary (bereavement) or permanent (cognitive impairment).
  • Firms must adapt communications/support to ensure good outcomes.
Exam Tip

If a scenario hints vulnerability (age, illness, grief, low literacy), correct answer usually involves slowing down, confirming understanding, offering support, documenting.

3. Status Disclosure

  • Must disclose whether advice is independent or restricted (where applicable).
  • Must disclose services, scope, and charges clearly before providing advice.

4. Client Money

  • Client money must be segregated (CASS rules) and protected from firm creditors.
  • Accurate reconciliation and controls are required.

5. Suitability Requirements

  • Advice must be suitable for the client’s objectives, circumstances, and risk profile.
  • Requires adequate factfind + risk profiling + explanation of why recommended solution fits.
Exam Trap

No factfind = no suitability. If info is insufficient, adviser must not recommend; must gather more info.

6. Best Execution vs Execution-Only

Best execution
  • Firm must take all sufficient steps to obtain best possible result for client
  • Relevant for dealing/trading services
Execution-only
  • No advice given
  • Client chooses product/instrument
  • Still must give clear disclosures and ensure communications not misleading

7. Cancellation rights

  • Some regulated products have cooling-off/cancellation periods.
  • Client must receive clear information about cancellation and consequences.

8–9. Product disclosure + PRIIPs / KIDs

  • Clients must get key information in good time before commitment.
  • PRIIPs: packaged retail and insurance-based investment products require a KID (Key Information Document).
  • KID summarises: what product is, risk/return, costs, recommended holding period, complaints.

10. Inducements & Adviser Charging

  • Inducements must not conflict with duty to act in client’s best interests.
  • For retail investment advice, commission is generally banned; adviser charging is agreed with client.

11. Conflicts of Interest

  • Firms must identify, manage, and disclose conflicts.
  • Example: recommending in-house products because it pays higher fees.
Exam Trap

“Disclosure” alone may not be enough — conflicts must be managed. If cannot be managed, avoid the activity.

12. Record Keeping

  • Keep records to evidence suitability and compliance.
  • Exam-style rule-of-thumb: investment advice records often 5 years; mortgage advice often 3 years.

13. Data Protection / GDPR

  • Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, transparently.
  • Only collect necessary data; keep secure; retain only as long as needed.
  • Breaches may require reporting to ICO within 72 hours (if risk to individuals).

14. Consumer Duty in action

  • Focus on measurable good outcomes (understanding, support, fair value).
  • Especially relevant for vulnerable customers.

15. Treating Customers Fairly (TCF) revisited

  • TCF is embedded in Principles for Businesses; Consumer Duty strengthens it.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. Which client type receives the highest level of FCA protection?
A. Eligible counterparty
B. Professional client
C. Retail client
D. None
Reveal answer
Answer: C — Retail clients receive the highest protection.
Q2. What is the key output of PRIIPs regulation for retail packaged products?
A. Mortgage illustration
B. KID (Key Information Document)
C. Share certificate
D. Credit agreement
Reveal answer
Answer: B — PRIIPs requires a KID for packaged retail and insurance-based investment products.
Q3. An adviser recommends a product without completing a factfind. Which rule is most directly breached?
A. Suitability requirements
B. Best execution
C. Market abuse
D. Basel III
Reveal answer
Answer: A — Without sufficient information, advice cannot be shown to be suitable.
