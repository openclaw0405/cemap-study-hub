17. Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

CGT is charged on the profit made when a chargeable asset is disposed of (sold, gifted, or transferred at arm's length).

Asset typeBasic-rate taxpayerHigher/Additional-rate taxpayer
Most assets (shares, investment property, etc.)10%20%
Residential property (non-main home)18%24%
Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR)10% (lifetime limit £1,000,000)
  • Annual Exempt Amount 2024/25: £3,000 — gains up to this are free of CGT
  • CGT is assessed on gains above the exempt amount after deducting allowable costs (purchase price, improvement costs, incidental disposal costs)
  • Losses in the same year are offset against gains before the annual exempt amount
Exam Trap: If the taxpayer's total income plus the gain straddles the basic/higher rate boundary, the gain is split — the part within the basic-rate band is taxed at the lower rate.

18. Avoiding CGT — Legally

The following disposals or assets are exempt from CGT:

Exempt assets
  • Principal Private Residence (PPR) — main home (full exemption if lived in throughout ownership)
  • ISA holdings (all growth and income is tax-free)
  • National Savings & Investments products
  • UK Government Gilts (fixed-interest securities)
  • Qualifying Life Assurance Policies
  • Cars (private use vehicles)
  • Chattel wasting assets — assets with ≤50 years useful life (e.g., racehorses)
  • Non-wasting chattels worth ≤£6,000 at disposal (e.g., antique chair)
Legal planning strategies
  • Bed and ISA — sell shares, rebuy inside ISA; uses annual exempt amount
  • Spousal transfers — transfers between spouses/civil partners are no gain/no loss
  • Timing disposals — spread gains over two tax years to use two annual exemptions
  • Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS) — CGT deferral relief and disposal exemption after 3 years

19. Inheritance Tax (IHT)

IHT is charged on the value of a deceased person's estate above the available threshold, and on certain lifetime gifts.

Thresholds 2024/25
  • Nil Rate Band (NRB): £325,000
  • Residence Nil Rate Band (RNRB): £175,000 — applies when main home passes to direct descendants
  • Maximum combined threshold: £500,000 (individual) or up to £1,000,000 (married couple — both allowances transferable)
  • RNRB tapers by £1 for every £2 of estate value above £2,000,000
IHT Rates
  • Standard rate: 40% above available threshold
  • Reduced rate: 36% if ≥10% of net estate left to charity
  • Lifetime gifts to trusts: 20% (chargeable lifetime transfer, CLT)

Gifts and the 7-Year Rule

  • Potentially Exempt Transfer (PET): gifts to individuals — become fully exempt if donor survives 7 years
  • If donor dies within 7 years, PET becomes chargeable — taper relief reduces the tax from year 3 onwards (40%→32%→24%→16%→8%→0%)

21. Value Added Tax (VAT)

VAT is a tax on the supply of goods and services. UK VAT registration threshold: £90,000 of taxable turnover (2024/25).

Standard Rate: 20%
  • Most goods and services
  • Business can reclaim input VAT
Reduced Rate: 5%
  • Domestic fuel and power
  • Energy-saving materials (insulation, solar panels)
  • Child car seats
  • Contraceptive products
  • Mobility aids for elderly
Zero-Rated: 0%
  • Food (not restaurant/hot takeaway)
  • Children's clothing and footwear
  • Books and newspapers
  • Prescription medicines
  • Public transport fares
  • Business can still reclaim input VAT
Exempt: no VAT
  • Financial services (loans, deposits, insurance)
  • Insurance (note: IPT applies instead)
  • Education and health
  • Most residential land and property
  • Business CANNOT reclaim input VAT
Exam Trap — Zero-rated vs Exempt: Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0% — the business can reclaim input VAT and must register if over the threshold. Exempt supplies are outside the VAT system — no input VAT recovery. This is a key distinction tested repeatedly.

22. Insurance Premium Tax (IPT)

  • IPT is charged on premiums for general insurance contracts sold in the UK
  • Standard rate: 12% — most general insurance (home, car, pet, liability)
  • Higher rate: 20% — travel insurance, mechanical or electrical appliance insurance, some vehicle insurance sold by finance dealers
  • Exempt from IPT: long-term life assurance, income protection insurance, permanent health insurance, commercial ships and aircraft insurance, reinsurance
Key fact: IPT is paid by the insurer and passed on via premiums. It is not VAT and cannot be reclaimed by VAT-registered businesses.

23. Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT)

SDLT applies on the purchase of land and property in England and Northern Ireland. It is a tiered (slice) tax — each rate applies only to the portion of price within that band.

Purchase price band (residential)Standard rateAdditional dwelling surcharge
£0 – £250,0000%+3%
£250,001 – £925,0005%+3%
£925,001 – £1,500,00010%+3%
Above £1,500,00012%+3%
First-Time Buyer Relief
  • 0% on first £425,000
  • 5% on £425,001 – £625,000
  • No relief if purchase price above £625,000
Additional Dwellings
  • +3% surcharge on all bands if buyer already owns another property
  • Applies to second homes, buy-to-let purchases
  • Refundable if main home sold within 3 years
Scotland uses LBTT (Land and Buildings Transaction Tax); Wales uses LTT (Land Transaction Tax). SDLT applies in England and Northern Ireland only.

24. Corporation Tax

  • Charged on the profits of UK companies (and some other incorporated bodies)
  • Tax year aligns with the company's accounting period (not 6 April – 5 April)
ProfitsRate 2024/25
£50,000 or below19% (Small Profits Rate)
£50,001 – £250,000Marginal relief (effective rate between 19% and 25%)
Above £250,00025% (Main Rate)
  • Corporation tax is paid 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period (or quarterly for very large companies)
  • Companies may deduct allowable business expenses, capital allowances, and losses before calculating taxable profits

Memorise

CGT Key Facts
  • Annual exempt amount: £3,000
  • Main home (PPR): fully exempt
  • ISAs, gilts, cars: exempt
  • Residential property rates: 18% / 24%
  • Other assets: 10% / 20%
IHT Key Figures
  • NRB: £325,000
  • RNRB: £175,000 (direct descendants + main home)
  • Rate: 40% (36% with charity gift)
  • 7-year PET rule; taper from year 3
  • Annual exemption: £3,000
VAT Categories
  • Standard: 20%
  • Reduced: 5% (fuel, energy-saving)
  • Zero-rated: 0% (food, books, children's clothes) — CAN reclaim input VAT
  • Exempt (financial, insurance) — CANNOT reclaim
SDLT & IPT
  • SDLT: 0% / 5% / 10% / 12% (slice system)
  • FTB relief: 0% to £425,000; capped at £625,000
  • Additional dwelling: +3%
  • IPT: standard 12%, higher 20% (travel)
  • Corp Tax: 19% (≤£50k) / 25% (≥£250k)

Practice Questions

Q1. A higher-rate taxpayer sells a buy-to-let property making a chargeable gain of £45,000 in 2024/25. She has no other chargeable gains. What CGT is payable?
A. £8,400 — 20% on the gain above the annual exempt amount
B. £10,080 — 24% on the gain above the £3,000 annual exempt amount
C. £7,200 — 18% on the full gain
D. £9,000 — 20% on the full £45,000
Reveal answer
Answer: B — £10,080. Residential property gains for a higher-rate taxpayer: 24%. Gain £45,000 minus annual exempt amount £3,000 = £42,000 taxable gain. £42,000 Ã 24% = £10,080.
Q2. Which of the following is EXEMPT from Insurance Premium Tax (IPT)?
A. Home contents insurance
B. Travel insurance
C. Pet insurance
D. Term life assurance
Reveal answer
Answer: D — Term life assurance. Long-term life assurance contracts (including term assurance, income protection and permanent health insurance) are exempt from IPT. Travel insurance attracts the higher IPT rate of 20%.
Q3. A first-time buyer purchases a property in England for £500,000. How much SDLT is payable?
A. £0 — fully covered by first-time buyer relief
B. £3,750 — 5% on the portion above £425,000
C. £12,500 — 5% on the full purchase price
D. £17,500 — standard rates apply as FTB relief does not apply above £425,000
Reveal answer
Answer: B — £3,750. FTB relief: 0% on £0–£425,000 (= £0). 5% on £425,001–£500,000 (= £75,000 Ã 5% = £3,750). Total = £3,750. Note: if the price exceeded £625,000, no FTB relief would apply at all.
