25. National Insurance (NI) 2024/25

NI contributions fund the State Pension, NHS, and certain benefits. There are several classes:

ClassWho paysRate / threshold 2024/25
Class 1 — employeeEmployees8% on earnings £12,570–£50,270; 2% above £50,270
Class 1 — employerEmployers13.8% on earnings above £9,100 (secondary threshold)
Class 1AEmployers13.8% on benefits in kind (P11D values)
Class 1BEmployers13.8% under a PAYE Settlement Agreement (PSA)
Class 2Self-employed£3.45/week flat rate; only if profits ≥ £12,570 (small profits threshold £6,725)
Class 3Voluntary£17.45/week — to fill NI record gaps
Class 4Self-employed6% on profits £12,570–£50,270; 2% above
Self-employed NI: A self-employed person pays both Class 2 (flat rate) AND Class 4 (profit-based). An employee only pays Class 1 (employee). An employer also pays Class 1 (employer side) — this is an additional cost on top of the employee's NI.
Exam Trap: Class 3 is voluntary — it does not entitle the contributor to any benefits other than filling gaps in their NI record for State Pension purposes.

26. Economic and Monetary Policy

Monetary Policy
  • Controlled by the Bank of England MPC
  • Tool: Bank Rate (the interest rate the BoE charges commercial banks)
  • Target: keep CPI inflation at 2%
  • Higher Bank Rate → more expensive to borrow → less spending → lower inflation
  • Lower Bank Rate → cheaper borrowing → more spending → stimulates growth
  • Quantitative Easing (QE): BoE buys government gilts from banks, injecting money into the economy; used when rates already near zero
Fiscal Policy
  • Controlled by HM Treasury / Chancellor
  • Tools: taxation and government spending
  • Expansionary: cut taxes / increase spending → stimulates economy → may increase inflation
  • Contractionary: raise taxes / cut spending → reduces demand → may reduce inflation
  • Budget deficit: government spends more than it receives in tax; funded by borrowing (gilts)
  • Supply-side policy: deregulation, privatisation, skills training — aims to increase productive capacity without inflation

27. Inflation

CPI — Consumer Prices Index
  • The official UK inflation measure
  • BoE target: 2%
  • Measures a basket of goods and services consumed by typical household
  • Excludes mortgage interest payments and council tax
  • Used to set interest rates and some benefit/pension uprating
RPI — Retail Prices Index
  • Older measure; generally higher than CPI
  • Includes mortgage interest payments and council tax
  • Used for: index-linked gilts, Rail fares, some student loan interest, some private pension increases
  • No longer a National Statistic — CPIH (CPI + owner-occupied housing costs) is now the ONS preferred measure

Causes of Inflation

Demand-pull
  • Too much money chasing too few goods
  • Caused by: low interest rates, high consumer confidence, government stimulus, rising wages
  • Solution: raise Bank Rate, reduce government spending
Cost-push
  • Rising production costs force businesses to raise prices
  • Caused by: oil price rises, supply chain disruption, rising import costs (weak £)
  • Solution: more difficult — raising rates may worsen the problem

Effects of Inflation

  • Savers: purchasing power of savings erodes — real return = nominal rate minus inflation
  • Borrowers: benefit — real value of debt falls over time
  • Fixed-income investors: hurt — coupon payments buy less over time
  • Mortgages: high inflation → BoE raises Bank Rate → variable rate mortgages become more expensive

28. Government Economic Policy

  • Fiscal policy is set in the Autumn Statement / Budget — the Chancellor announces tax changes and spending plans
  • OBR (Office for Budget Responsibility) independently forecasts UK economic performance and the cost of fiscal measures
  • National Debt = cumulative government borrowing; funded by issuing gilts; interest cost must be serviced from tax receipts
  • Automatic stabilisers: welfare payments increase in recession (higher unemployment benefits) and tax receipts fall — naturally stimulating the economy without active policy decisions
  • Exchange rate policy: a stronger pound makes imports cheaper (reduces cost-push inflation) but makes exports more expensive (may reduce growth)
Relevance to mortgages: Interest rate expectations, inflation, and government spending directly affect mortgage rates, house prices, and client affordability. CeMAP advisers must understand this macro context.

29. Welfare Benefits

Key benefits relevant to mortgage advisers assessing client income:

BenefitKey facts
Universal Credit (UC)Replaces 6 legacy benefits: Income Support, JSA, ESA, Housing Benefit, Working Tax Credit, Child Tax Credit. Monthly payment. Earnings taper: 55p in £1 withdrawn above work allowance.
State Pension£221.20/week (2024/25 new State Pension). Requires 35 qualifying years of NI for full pension; minimum 10 years to receive any pension. Paid from State Pension Age (currently 66 for men and women).
Child Benefit£25.60/week for first child; £16.95/week for each additional child (2024/25). Subject to High Income Child Benefit Charge (HICBC).
Housing BenefitBeing replaced by UC. Helps low-income renters with rental costs. Now mostly claimed via UC.
Attendance AllowanceFor disabled people aged 65 or over. Not means tested. Two rates based on care needs.
Personal Independence Payment (PIP)Replaces Disability Living Allowance (DLA) for working-age claimants (16–64). Not means tested. Two components: daily living and mobility. Two rates each (standard/enhanced).
Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA)Legacy benefit being replaced by UC. Contribution-based JSA paid for up to 6 months.
High Income Child Benefit Charge (HICBC): Where either parent has adjusted net income above £60,000, the charge claws back Child Benefit at 1% for each £200 above £60,000. At £80,000 income, 100% of Child Benefit is clawed back via self-assessment.

30. Welfare Summary

Means-tested benefits (based on income/savings)
  • Universal Credit
  • Pension Credit
  • Council Tax Reduction
  • Free school meals
Contributory / NI-based benefits (based on NI record)
  • New State Pension
  • Contribution-based JSA (6 months max)
  • Contribution-based ESA
  • Bereavement benefits
Universal benefits (not means-tested, not NI-based)
  • Child Benefit (subject to HICBC)
  • Attendance Allowance
  • Personal Independence Payment (PIP)
  • Carer's Allowance
State Pension key numbers
  • Full amount: £221.20/week (2024/25)
  • Full pension: 35 qualifying years
  • Minimum pension: 10 qualifying years
  • State Pension Age: 66
  • Deferral: pension increases by 1% for every 9 weeks deferred

Practice Questions

Q1. Maria is self-employed with profits of £35,000 in 2024/25. Which National Insurance classes must she pay?
A. Class 1 employee only
B. Class 2 and Class 4
C. Class 1 and Class 3
D. Class 4 only — Class 2 is no longer payable
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Class 2 and Class 4. Self-employed individuals with profits above the small profits threshold (£6,725) pay Class 2 (£3.45/week) AND Class 4 (6% on profits £12,570–£50,270; 2% above). They do NOT pay Class 1 (that is for employees).
Q2. Universal Credit replaced six legacy benefits. Which of the following was NOT one of the six?
A. Housing Benefit
B. Child Tax Credit
C. Attendance Allowance
D. Working Tax Credit
Reveal answer
Answer: C — Attendance Allowance. Attendance Allowance is a universal (non-means-tested) benefit for people aged 65+ with care needs. It was not replaced by UC. The six UC legacy benefits are: Income Support, JSA, ESA, Housing Benefit, Working Tax Credit, and Child Tax Credit.
Q3. The Bank of England raises the Bank Rate from 4.5% to 5.25%. What is the most likely direct effect on UK mortgage holders with tracker mortgages?
A. Monthly payments fall immediately as lenders pass on reduced funding costs
B. Monthly payments rise immediately as tracker rates follow Bank Rate
C. No immediate effect — tracker mortgages are only reviewed annually
D. The effect depends on whether inflation is demand-pull or cost-push
Reveal answer
Answer: B. Tracker mortgages are set at a fixed margin above the Bank Rate (e.g., Bank Rate + 1%). An increase in Bank Rate immediately increases the monthly payment. This is a key reason why the BoE's monetary policy decisions directly affect mortgage affordability and why mortgage advisers must understand interest rate dynamics.
