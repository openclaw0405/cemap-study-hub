1. Introduction

Consumer credit is lending to individuals (or small entities) for personal purposes. It includes credit cards, personal loans, hire purchase, and certain secured credit arrangements.

2. Consumer Credit Act (CCA) 1974

  • Core statute regulating many consumer credit agreements.
  • Provides rules on agreements, disclosure, advertising, settlement, default, and enforcement.
  • Historically applied strongly to agreements up to certain limits; now FCA rules sit alongside.
Exam Tip

In modern exams, remember: FCA regulates consumer credit firms (CONC). CCA still matters for agreement structure and consumer rights.

3. Regulation of Consumer Credit (FCA CONC)

  • FCA took over consumer credit regulation from the OFT (Office of Fair Trading) in 2014.
  • FCA rules for consumer credit are in CONC (Consumer Credit sourcebook).
  • Focus: affordability checks, fair treatment, clear communications, forbearance in arrears, vulnerable customers.

4. Consumer Credit Directives

  • EU-driven rules historically shaped UK consumer credit disclosure and rights.
  • Standardised information, APR concepts, early settlement rights.

5. Regulated Mortgage Contract (definition)

  • A regulated mortgage contract is generally a loan secured on land in the UK where at least 40% of the property is used as a dwelling by the borrower or related person.
  • Borrower is an individual or trustee.
  • Regulated by FCA under MCOB rules.
Exam Trap

Don’t confuse a regulated mortgage with any secured loan. The “dwelling” requirement is key. Commercial loans secured on commercial property are usually not regulated mortgages.

6. Credit Broking

  • Credit broking = introducing or arranging credit for a consumer.
  • It is a regulated activity for consumer credit and must comply with FCA rules.
Kenneth’s Angle

Think of a broker like an originator: even if they don’t lend money, their conduct shapes mis-selling risk. That is why credit broking is regulated.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. Which FCA sourcebook contains consumer credit conduct rules?
A. COBS
B. MCOB
C. CONC
D. CASS
Reveal answer
Answer: C — CONC is the Consumer Credit sourcebook.
Q2. Which feature is essential for a regulated mortgage contract?
A. Any loan over £25,000
B. Secured on land with dwelling use by borrower/related person
C. Interest-only repayment
D. Must be a business loan
Reveal answer
Answer: B — It must be secured on land and meet the dwelling/occupancy condition.
Q3. Credit broking refers to:
A. Setting the Bank of England base rate
B. Introducing or arranging credit for a consumer
C. Paying FSCS compensation
D. Underwriting mortgages only
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Introducing/arranging credit is credit broking and is regulated.
