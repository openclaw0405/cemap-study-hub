1. Complaints

  • A complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction (oral or written) about a firm’s service or product.
  • Firms must have a documented complaints procedure and handle complaints fairly, consistently, promptly.
  • Clients must be told how to complain and what happens next.
Common exam timeline
  • Acknowledge promptly
  • Investigate and provide a final response within 8 weeks
  • If not resolved, client can escalate to FOS
What a final response should include
  • Decision + reasons
  • Any redress offered
  • FOS referral rights + time limits
Exam Trap

FOS is usually only approached AFTER the firm has had a chance to resolve internally. Many questions test “what happens next?” — internal procedure first, then FOS.

2. The Ombudsman (FOS)

  • FOS = Financial Ombudsman Service.
  • Independent dispute resolution for eligible complainants.
  • Free to consumers; firms pay case fees.
  • Can order redress; decisions can be binding on firms (if consumer accepts).
Exam Tip

If the firm is still trading and the issue is mis-selling / poor service, think FOS. If the firm has failed, think FSCS.

3. The FSCS

  • FSCS = Financial Services Compensation Scheme.
  • Compensation when an authorised firm is unable to meet claims (usually because it has failed).
  • Funded by levies on regulated firms.
Key numbers
  • Deposits: £85,000 per person per institution
  • Investment: typically £85,000 (exam often focuses on the number)
Not covered
  • Losses from normal market movement (investment value falling)
  • Some non-regulated products / scams
  • P2P lending losses are often tested as not FSCS protected
Kenneth’s Angle

Think of FSCS as “creditor protection after default”: it only triggers when the firm cannot pay. FOS is “dispute resolution while the firm still exists”.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. A client is unhappy with an adviser’s service. What is the correct first step?
A. Go straight to FSCS
B. Go straight to FOS without contacting the firm
C. Use the firm’s internal complaints procedure first
D. Call the Bank of England
Reveal answer
Answer: C — Internal complaints procedure first, then FOS if unresolved.
Q2. What does FSCS primarily do?
A. Sets interest rates
B. Resolves disputes between clients and firms
C. Pays compensation when an authorised firm fails and cannot meet claims
D. Approves financial promotions
Reveal answer
Answer: C — FSCS pays compensation when firms fail.
Q3. What is the FSCS deposit protection limit per person per institution?
A. £50,000
B. £85,000
C. £100,000
D. Unlimited
Reveal answer
Answer: B — £85,000 per person per institution.
