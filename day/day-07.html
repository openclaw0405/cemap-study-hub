17. Enterprise Investment Schemes (EIS)

Government scheme to encourage investment in smaller, higher-risk, unquoted trading companies. High risk — not suitable for most clients.

FeatureDetail
Income tax relief30% of amount invested, deducted from income tax bill. Max investment: £1,000,000/year (£2m for knowledge-intensive companies).
Minimum holding period3 years — if sold sooner, income tax relief is clawed back
CGT deferralA chargeable gain from any asset can be deferred by reinvesting in EIS shares within 1 year before or 3 years after the gain
CGT exemptionGains on EIS shares themselves are CGT-exempt if held ≥3 years AND income tax relief was claimed
Loss reliefLosses on EIS shares (net of income tax relief) can be offset against income or capital gains
Dividend incomeTaxable — no exemption on dividends from EIS companies
IHT Business ReliefEIS shares in qualifying trading companies: 100% relief from IHT after 2 years
Exam Trap: EIS income tax relief is 30%, not 20% or 40%. The £2m limit only applies to 'knowledge-intensive companies'. Dividends from EIS are taxable. The holding period for income tax relief is 3 years (vs VCT which is 5 years).

18. Venture Capital Trusts (VCTs)

Listed investment trusts that hold a portfolio of unquoted/AIM-listed companies. More diversified than EIS.

FeatureDetail
Income tax relief30% of amount invested. Max investment: £200,000/year.
Minimum holding period5 years — if sold sooner, income tax relief is clawed back
Dividend incomeTax-free — unlike EIS, VCT dividends are exempt from income tax
Capital gainsGains on VCT shares are CGT-exempt — no CGT on disposal of VCT shares
CGT deferralNOT available for VCTs — unlike EIS, no CGT deferral relief
IHTListed VCT shares do NOT qualify for Business Relief (they are listed)

19. Stock Markets

Primary Market
  • Companies issue new shares to raise capital
  • IPO (Initial Public Offering): first-time listing
  • Rights issue: additional shares offered to existing holders
  • Proceeds go to the company
Secondary Market
  • Investors trade existing shares between each other
  • Proceeds go to the seller, NOT the company
  • Provides liquidity for shareholders
London Stock Exchange (LSE)
  • Main Market: large, established companies; stringent listing requirements; includes FTSE 100/250
  • AIM: Alternative Investment Market; smaller, growing companies; less regulation; higher risk; AIM shares can qualify for EIS/IHT relief
Market Mechanics
  • Market makers: quote bid (buy) and offer (sell) prices; provide liquidity
  • Bid/offer spread: market maker's profit
  • Settlement: T+2 (2 business days after trade)
  • SETS: electronic order book for FTSE stocks

20. Real Estate

Direct investment in physical property — the most tangible asset class.

Residential Property
  • Buy-to-let: rental income + capital gain potential
  • Higher SDLT: surcharge on second homes (3%)
  • Mortgage interest: limited tax relief for individuals (Section 24 — basic rate credit only)
  • CGT on disposal (minus PPR if ever lived in)
  • HMO (Houses in Multiple Occupation): higher rental yield, more regulation
Commercial Property
  • Offices, retail, industrial units
  • Typically long leases (5–25 years)
  • Yield usually higher than residential
  • More sensitive to economic cycles
  • VAT may apply on purchase/rental

Characteristics of direct property investment: illiquid (hard to sell quickly), high transaction costs (SDLT, legal fees, agent fees), needs management, undiversified, but provides rental income + inflation hedge over long term.

21. Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

Listed companies that own income-producing real estate. Combines property exposure with stock market liquidity.

FeatureDetail
Distribution requirementMust distribute at least 90% of rental profits to shareholders each year
Tax exemptionExempt from corporation tax on qualifying rental profits and gains from property sales
Property Income Distributions (PIDs)Dividends from the ring-fenced rental profits. Taxed as property income (not dividends) — usually paid with 20% basic rate tax deducted at source
Non-PID dividendsFrom non-property activities — taxed as ordinary dividends
ISA/pension eligibleREIT shares can be held in an ISA or SIPP; within these wrappers PIDs receive gross treatment
ListingMust be listed on a recognised stock exchange (liquid, unlike direct property)
Exam Trap: REIT PIDs are taxed as property income, NOT dividend income. This means the dividend allowance does NOT apply to PIDs, and they are liable to income tax at the investor's marginal rate (after the 20% withheld at source credit).

22. ESG — Green Investing

ESG stands for Environmental, Social, and Governance. It integrates non-financial factors into investment decisions.

Environmental
  • Climate change & carbon emissions
  • Renewable energy use
  • Water management
  • Biodiversity impact
Social
  • Employee welfare and diversity
  • Supply chain ethics
  • Community relations
  • Human rights
Governance
  • Board diversity & independence
  • Executive pay transparency
  • Anti-corruption policies
  • Shareholder rights

Investment Approaches

  • Negative screening: exclude sectors (tobacco, weapons, fossil fuels, gambling)
  • Positive screening: invest in best-in-class ESG performers in each sector
  • ESG integration: incorporate ESG analysis into standard financial analysis
  • Impact investing: direct investment to achieve measurable positive outcomes
  • Stewardship/engagement: use shareholder influence to improve company ESG practices
Greenwashing: misleadingly portraying a product as more environmentally friendly than it is. A key regulatory concern — FCA has issued guidance on sustainability disclosure requirements (SDR).

23. Foreign Exchange

  • Spot rate: exchange rate for immediate (today) delivery of currency
  • Forward rate: agreed exchange rate for a future date — used to hedge currency risk (locks in a known rate)
  • Exchange rate quotation: £/$ = number of dollars per £1. If £/$= 1.26, £1 buys $1.26.
  • Sterling weakens: imports become more expensive (inflationary) → cost-push inflation pressure; exports become cheaper (competitive advantage)
  • Sterling strengthens: imports cheaper; exports more expensive (less competitive)

Factors affecting exchange rates

  • Interest rate differentials: higher rates attract foreign capital → stronger currency
  • Inflation: high inflation → currency depreciates relative to lower-inflation countries
  • Trade balance: surplus → demand for £ → stronger pound
  • Political stability and government policy
  • Speculation and market sentiment
Exam relevance: Currency risk applies to any overseas investment. A UK investor holding US equities gains if $ strengthens against £ and loses if $ weakens. Offshore accounts and EIS in overseas companies carry currency risk.

24. Unit Trusts

An authorised collective investment scheme — pools investors' money to invest in a diversified portfolio.

Structure
  • Open-ended: units created when investors buy; cancelled when they sell
  • Trustee: legal owner of assets; independent of fund manager
  • Fund manager: makes investment decisions under the trust deed
  • Regulated by FCA under COLL rules
Pricing
  • Dual pricing: manager quotes a higher offer price (buy) and lower bid price (sell)
  • Bid/offer spread = fund manager's initial charge
  • Price = NAV of underlying assets Ã· units, adjusted for charges
  • Dilution levy: sometimes charged to protect existing investors
Income Units vs Accumulation Units
  • Income units: income (dividends) distributed to unitholders
  • Accumulation units: income automatically reinvested → unit price rises
Charges
  • Initial charge (front-end load): e.g., up to 5%
  • Annual Management Charge (AMC): e.g., 0.75%/year
  • TER (Total Expense Ratio): all ongoing charges including AMC + admin
  • OCF (Ongoing Charges Figure): now preferred term

Practice Questions

Q1. An investor puts £50,000 into an EIS-qualifying company in 2024/25 and holds the shares for 4 years before selling at a gain. Which statement is correct?
A. The investor receives 40% income tax relief and the gain on disposal is fully taxable
B. The investor receives 30% income tax relief; dividends received are tax-free; the gain on disposal is CGT-exempt
C. The investor receives 30% income tax relief; dividends received are taxable; the gain on disposal is CGT-exempt (as held >3 years)
D. The investor receives 20% income tax relief; the full gain is subject to CGT at 10%
Reveal answer
Answer: C. EIS income tax relief is 30% (not 20% or 40%). Dividends from EIS companies are taxable as normal. However, gains on disposal of EIS shares are CGT-exempt provided the shares have been held for at least 3 years AND income tax relief was originally claimed. The holding period here (4 years) exceeds the minimum 3-year requirement.
Q2. A UK REIT pays a Property Income Distribution (PID) to an individual investor. How is this distribution taxed?
A. As dividend income — eligible for the dividend allowance
B. As property income — basic rate tax (20%) deducted at source; investor declares balance on self-assessment
C. As capital gain — taxed at 18% or 28% depending on total income
D. Tax-free — REITs are exempt from all UK taxes on distributions
Reveal answer
Answer: B. PIDs from REITs are classified as property income, NOT dividend income. This means the dividend allowance does not apply. The REIT deducts 20% basic rate tax at source. Higher-rate and additional-rate taxpayers declare the PID on self-assessment and pay the additional tax. Within an ISA or SIPP, PIDs are received gross and shelter entirely from tax.
Q3. Compared to investing directly in commercial property, which advantage do REIT shares specifically offer?
A. REITs are exempt from all income tax on distributions
B. REITs provide liquidity — shares can be bought and sold on the stock exchange
C. REITs are not subject to stock market volatility, unlike shares
D. REIT shares qualify for 100% IHT Business Relief after 2 years
Reveal answer
Answer: B — liquidity. Direct commercial property is illiquid — it can take months to buy or sell. REIT shares are listed on a stock exchange and can be traded throughout the day, providing much greater liquidity. Note: REITs can still experience volatility as listed companies (C is wrong). REITs pay income that is taxable (A is wrong). Listed REIT shares do NOT qualify for IHT Business Relief (D is wrong).
