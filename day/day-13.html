1. Current Regulation (FCA + PRA)

PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority)
  • Part of Bank of England
  • Focus: safety and soundness of firms
  • Covers: banks/building societies/credit unions, insurers, major investment firms
FCA (Financial Conduct Authority)
  • Focus: conduct + consumer outcomes + market integrity
  • Regulates most retail financial services firms
  • Sets detailed rulebooks (COBS/MCOB/CONC etc.)
Exam Trap

Don’t mix PRA/FCA roles: PRA = prudential (capital, resilience). FCA = conduct (treat customers fairly, disclosure, sales practices).

2. EU Impact on UK Regulation

  • Historically: many UK rules were shaped by EU directives/regulations (e.g. MiFID, Mortgage Credit Directive).
  • Post-Brexit: rules remain largely in place, but UK can amend independently (FCA still enforces UK versions).

3. Main UK Legislation

  • FSMA 2000: core framework for regulation; defines regulated activities/authorisation.
  • Supporting legislation & secondary rules: FCA Handbook, statutory instruments.
  • Data protection: UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 (covered later in Ch10).

4. Regulatory Aims & Objectives (FCA)

  • Consumer protection
  • Market integrity
  • Competition in the interests of consumers
Exam Tip

If a question asks “what is FCA trying to achieve?”, these 3 are the go-to answers.

5. FCA Scope & Powers (high level)

  • Authorisation, supervision, enforcement, rule-making.
  • Can fine firms/individuals; restrict permissions; require redress programs.

6. FCA Principles for Businesses (what they feel like)

  • Act with integrity
  • Act with due skill, care and diligence
  • Management and control (systems/controls)
  • Customers’ interests (treat them fairly)
  • Communications must be clear, fair and not misleading
Kenneth’s Angle

Think like credit risk: prudential rules stop firms collapsing; conduct rules stop mis-selling. Exams test whether you know which bucket a rule sits in.

7. Prudential Regulation (why it exists)

  • Ensures firms hold enough capital/liquidity to survive shocks.
  • Prevents systemic failures (2008-style contagion).

8. Basel Accords (Made Simple)

  • International banking standards for capital adequacy & risk management.
  • Basel III strengthened capital and introduced liquidity requirements after 2008.

9. Fair Treatment of Customers (TCF)

  • FCA expects fair outcomes across the customer journey (sales → after-sales support → complaints).
  • Linked to Principle 6 (Customers’ interests).

10. Consumer Duty (2023)

The Duty
  • Firms must deliver good outcomes for retail customers.
  • Higher standard than TCF (more measurable outcomes).
4 outcomes
  • Products & services
  • Price & value
  • Consumer understanding
  • Consumer support

11. How Customers are Protected

  • FOS: resolves complaints (free to consumer).
  • FSCS: compensation when authorised firms fail (key numbers covered earlier).

12. SM&CR (Senior Managers & Certification Regime)

  • Senior Managers: clear accountability + Statement of Responsibilities.
  • Certification: key roles assessed annually as fit & proper.
  • Conduct Rules apply broadly.
Exam Trap

SM&CR is about personal accountability. If something goes wrong, FCA can pursue individuals, not just the firm.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. Which regulator focuses on the safety and soundness of banks and insurers?
A. FCA
B. PRA
C. FOS
D. FSCS
Reveal answer
Answer: B — PRA is the prudential regulator under the Bank of England.
Q2. What are the FCA’s three operational objectives?
A. Growth, employment, stability
B. Consumer protection, market integrity, competition
C. Capital adequacy, liquidity, leverage
D. Disclosure, affordability, suitability
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Consumer protection, market integrity, competition.
Q3. Which of the following is a Consumer Duty outcome?
A. Capital buffers
B. Consumer understanding
C. Basel III compliance
D. Resolution planning
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Consumer understanding is one of the 4 Duty outcomes.
