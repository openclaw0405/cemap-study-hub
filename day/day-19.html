1. Money Laundering — what it is

  • Money laundering = disguising the origin of criminal property to make it appear legitimate.
  • Three classic stages: Placement → Layering → Integration.
Placement

Getting cash into the financial system (e.g., depositing cash, buying monetary instruments).

Layering

Complex transactions to obscure trail (multiple transfers, offshore accounts, crypto exchanges).

Integration

Reintroducing funds as apparently legitimate wealth (property purchase, businesses, investments).

2. Key AML Laws & Framework

  • Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (POCA): money laundering offences; failure to disclose.
  • Terrorism Act 2000: terrorist financing offences.
  • Money Laundering Regulations: CDD/EDD requirements for firms.

3. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

  • Identify and verify client identity (name, DOB) + address.
  • Understand purpose and intended nature of the relationship.
  • Ongoing monitoring: transactions must make sense vs client profile.
Exam Tip

CDD must be completed before establishing the business relationship / carrying out transactions (subject to limited exceptions).

4. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

  • Applied to higher-risk clients: PEPs (politically exposed persons), complex structures, high-risk jurisdictions.
  • Deeper checks: source of wealth, source of funds, senior management approval, increased monitoring.

5. Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR) + MLRO

  • Firms must have an MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer).
  • If suspicion exists, staff report internally to MLRO; MLRO decides whether to file a SAR to the NCA.
  • Tipping off (telling the client a SAR was made) is a criminal offence.
Exam Trap

If you suspect money laundering: don’t confront the client. Follow internal process → MLRO → SAR to NCA. Never disclose that a SAR has been filed.

6. Data Protection (UK GDPR / DPA 2018)

  • Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, transparently.
  • Keep data secure; limit access; keep only what you need; retain only as long as necessary.
  • Individuals have rights: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability.
Breach reporting

Report to ICO within 72 hours if the breach is likely to risk individuals’ rights/freedoms.

Practical controls

Encryption, strong passwords, least-privilege access, secure disposal, incident logs.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. What are the three classic stages of money laundering?
A. Lending, borrowing, default
B. Placement, layering, integration
C. Deposit, withdrawal, transfer
D. Verify, approve, repay
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Placement → Layering → Integration.
Q2. If an adviser suspects money laundering, what should they do?
A. Tell the client you are reporting them
B. Report internally to the MLRO and follow SAR process
C. Ignore it unless certain
D. Postpone and warn colleagues publicly
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Report to MLRO; MLRO may file SAR to NCA. Do not tip off.
Q3. Under GDPR, when should a serious data breach generally be reported to the ICO?
A. Within 24 hours
B. Within 72 hours
C. Within 30 days
D. Only if the client complains
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Report within 72 hours if risk to individuals’ rights/freedoms.
