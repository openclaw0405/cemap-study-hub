1. Financial Promotions

  • A financial promotion is any communication that invites or induces a person to engage in investment activity.
  • Rule: promotions must be clear, fair and not misleading.
  • Under FSMA s21, a promotion must be made by an authorised person or approved by one (unless an exemption applies).
Exam Trap

Students mix “regulated activity” and “promotion”. Even if a firm isn’t advising, a promotion can still be regulated. If it’s an invitation/inducement to engage, it needs proper approval.

2. Advertising

  • Advertising is a subset of promotions: brochures, websites, social posts, emails, paid ads.
  • Must not omit key facts that would change the impression.
  • Must present risks in a balanced way — not hide risk behind small print.
Exam Tip

If a question asks “what is wrong with this advert?”, look for: exaggerated benefits, missing risk warnings, misleading comparisons, unclear fees.

3. Reporting & Record Keeping

  • Firms must keep adequate records to evidence compliance, suitability, and client communications.
  • Records help with complaints, audits, and FCA supervision.
Typical exam numbers (rules-of-thumb)
  • Mortgage advice records: often tested as 3 years minimum (MCOB)
  • Investment advice records: commonly tested as 5 years minimum (COBS)
What to keep
  • Factfind + objectives
  • Risk profile / vulnerability notes
  • Suitability rationale + disclosures
  • Client communications and approvals

4. The Myth of CAS (CASS) — Client Assets

  • CASS = Client Assets Sourcebook (often misheard as “CAS”).
  • Purpose: protect client money and custody assets if a firm fails.
  • Key concept: segregation — client money held separate from firm money.
  • Client money rules reduce the risk that client funds are used to pay the firm’s creditors.
Exam Trap

CASS does not guarantee client money can never be lost. It sets protection rules (segregation, reconciliation). FSCS is the compensation scheme if an authorised firm fails.

5. Training & Competence (T&C)

  • Firms must ensure staff are competent for their role and remain competent.
  • New advisers must be supervised until competent.
  • Ongoing CPD (continuing professional development) is expected.
  • Competence includes technical knowledge + process compliance (factfind, disclosure, suitability).
Kenneth’s Angle

T&C is how regulators prevent “bad underwriting” in advice. Like credit committees: decisions must be consistent, documented, and made by qualified people.

Practice Questions (MCQ)

Q1. What is the key FCA standard for financial promotions?
A. Profitable, popular and persuasive
B. Clear, fair and not misleading
C. Short, simple and unregulated
D. Approved by FSCS
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Promotions must be clear, fair and not misleading.
Q2. CASS (Client Assets Sourcebook) is primarily about:
A. Setting interest rates
B. Segregating client money/custody assets from firm money
C. Paying compensation automatically
D. Regulating P2P lending
Reveal answer
Answer: B — CASS rules are about client asset protection through segregation and controls.
Q3. Under FSMA s21, who can approve a financial promotion (generally)?
A. Any marketing agency
B. An authorised person/firm
C. The FOS
D. The Bank of England only
Reveal answer
Answer: B — Generally, promotions must be made by or approved by an authorised person.
